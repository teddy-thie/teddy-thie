Overview

Alinkeo is a learning platform: courses built from video lessons, made to get knowledge to a specific group of people: military veterans. Authors upload a video and get a transcript with it, from human transcription or speech recognition. As senior designer, working with junior designer Mutala Musau, I designed the course player learners study in and the tools inside it.

Problem

Lessons are long videos. A video is hard to search, hard to skim and hard to come back to, and the questions a learner has are about one moment in it, not the whole lesson.

Outcome

A course player built around the transcript: search it, jump from any line, and attach a note, comment, question or pin to the exact moment it's about.

The challenge
a video is hard to come back to

A lesson can run past 20 minutes. Learners want to find the part where a term came up, keep a thought for later, or ask about the bit they didn't follow.

In a plain video player all of that happens somewhere else: a notebook, a forum, or scrubbing back and forth along the timeline. The work was to bring it into the player and tie it to the moment it's about.

Two passes
from tabs to a reading panel

The first pass put every tool in the side panel's tabs, with small text buttons in the player bar: Display Feedback Type and Drop A Pin.

The refined player gives the transcript room to be read. The side panel opens on it with search at the top, and note and pin sit in the player bar as icons beside speed, picture-in-picture and accessibility.

A working stand-in for the player. Search the transcript, click any line to jump there, then take a note, comment, ask a question or drop a pin on it. Everything you save takes you back to its moment.

    The design
    three decisions

    Each one moves something learners did outside the video into the player, and ties it to a time in the lesson.

    01

    The transcript is the way in

    CHOSE
    A searchable transcript beside the video, where any line jumps the video to that moment.
    INSTEAD OF
    The timeline as the only way to find a part of the lesson.
    WHY
    Words are faster to scan than a scrubber. Search finds every mention of a term and steps through them, "2 of 10".
    02

    Mark the moment, not the video

    CHOSE
    Select a passage and attach a note, comment, question or pin to its time stamp, such as 4:20 to 5:10.
    INSTEAD OF
    Notes and comments about the lesson as a whole.
    WHY
    Anything a learner saves can take them back to the second it's about. One menu, four choices, wherever the passage is.
    03

    Questions keep their moment

    CHOSE
    Q&A threads tied to a time in the lesson, with Play to rewatch it and the instructor's replies underneath.
    INSTEAD OF
    A general comments list under the course.
    WHY
    The instructor answering sees exactly what was asked about, and the next learner stuck on the same part finds the answer there.

    The model
    one line, many layers

    Every tool in the player hangs off the same thing: a time range in the transcript. That's what lets a note, a question or a pin take you back.

    For course authors
    the same transcript, cut into clips

    Authors upload a video or record one. In the editor, they select part of the transcript to make a clip, loop a section while they work on it, and add interactions along the timeline.

    The transcript learners read is the one authors edit with, so a moment means the same thing on both sides.

    Outcome
    what changed

    • A course player where the transcript is the way into every lesson, on desktop and mobile.
    • Notes, comments, questions and pins all attach to a moment, and all lead back to it.
    • Authors cut clips and build interactions from the same transcript learners read.
    • Shipped, for the veterans taking its courses.

    Next time
    what I'd do differently

    01

    Measure which tools get used

    Four ways to mark a moment is a lot. Usage per tool would show which ones earn a place up front and which can sit a tap further away.

    02

    Open with fewer tabs

    The refined player still opens with ten tabs across the side panel and below the video. I'd start with Transcript and Lessons and let learners add the rest with the + button.
